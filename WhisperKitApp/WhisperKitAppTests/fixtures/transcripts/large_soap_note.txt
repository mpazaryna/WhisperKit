Subjective: The patient is a 45-year-old male presenting with chief complaint of lower back pain radiating to the left leg for the past two weeks. Pain is described as sharp and shooting, rated 7 out of 10. Aggravating factors include prolonged sitting and bending forward. Relieving factors include lying down and ice application. Patient denies bowel or bladder dysfunction. No recent trauma reported.

Objective: Vital signs within normal limits. Gait is antalgic favoring the right side. Posture shows increased lumbar lordosis. Palpation reveals tenderness over L4-L5 and L5-S1 segments bilaterally. Muscle spasm noted in the left paraspinal muscles. Range of motion is limited in lumbar flexion to 40 degrees and extension to 10 degrees. Straight leg raise is positive on the left at 45 degrees. Neurological exam shows decreased sensation in the L5 dermatome on the left. Motor strength is 4 out of 5 in left ankle dorsiflexion.

Assessment: Lumbar radiculopathy, likely L5 nerve root involvement on the left. Rule out herniated nucleus pulposus at L4-L5 or L5-S1.

Plan: Recommend MRI of the lumbar spine. Initiate chiropractic care with spinal manipulation and flexion-distraction technique. Prescribe home exercises including McKenzie extensions and core stabilization. Follow up in one week to reassess. If symptoms worsen or red flags develop, refer to orthopedic spine specialist.
